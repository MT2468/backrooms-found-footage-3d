# Backrooms (2026) film research

Research target: Kane Parsons' 2026 A24 feature, not the unrelated community-wiki level canon.

## Confirmed production facts
- Directed by Kane Parsons; written by Will Soodik.
- Main cast: Chiwetel Ejiofor (Clark), Renate Reinsve (Mary), Mark Duplass (Phil), Finn Bennett (Bobby), Lukita Maxwell (Kat).
- Set in 1990.
- The official premise begins with a strange doorway in the basement of a furniture showroom.
- Parsons describes his Backrooms as a seemingly endless, non-game-like maze and emphasizes that the horror comes from bland ordinary spaces extrapolated infinitely rather than from making every room visually dark or conventionally horrific.
- The film was designed heavily in Blender before physical production and used roughly 30,000 square feet of built sets.

## Narrative spine reconstructed from official material and spoiler reviews
1. Prologue: found-footage/Async imagery establishes that people have already entered the impossible space and that something can pursue them there.
2. Clark is a failed architect, divorced, angry, drinking, and living in his failing furniture showroom, Cap'n Clark's Ottoman Empire. His pirate advertising persona embodies a humiliating version of the life he thinks he should have escaped.
3. Clark attends therapy with Dr. Mary Kline. Their repeated role-play of the night his wife expelled him reveals Clark's refusal to accept responsibility for his life.
4. Mary has her own unresolved childhood trauma: an agoraphobic/mentally ill mother, covered windows, confinement, later institutionalization, and a concrete handprint she saves from the demolished childhood home.
5. Flickering lights lead Clark to the store's lower level. After manipulating the breakers, he notices light through a wall and discovers he can pass through it.
6. First exploration: yellow wallpaper, stale carpet, fluorescent hum, impossible geometry, piled/misplaced furniture, embedded objects, reversed or incorrectly reproduced signs, distant movement and sound. Clark escapes but becomes fascinated rather than deterred.
7. Mary does not believe Clark's account, so he decides to document proof.
8. Clark recruits Kat and her boyfriend Bobby and brings them through the wall with a rented VHS camera.
9. The expedition reaches a steep/sloped opening. Bobby is tied to a rope and lowered with the camera into a lower area filled with clothing and a dark adjoining space.
10. Bobby sees a large entity and is pulled back toward the lower space. The failed rope rescue drags the group into danger. Bobby is killed; Clark and Kat are separated while fleeing through increasingly strange spaces.
11. Kat's exact death is intentionally ambiguous in the film, but the narrative strongly associates her disappearance with the same pursuing presence/Pirate Clark.
12. Clark remains in the Backrooms. He learns enough of its patterns to make a grotesque home/kingdom from copied rooms, furniture and passive malformed human facsimiles (Still Lifes). He grows attached to the dimension.
13. Mary enters while searching for Clark and eventually finds him. Clark explains his theory that the space rebuilds people and places from imperfect memories, retaining essence while corrupting details.
14. Clark forces Mary into another therapy role-play/dinner scene, wanting validation that he was not responsible for his failed marriage/life. Mary finally confronts him instead.
15. The gigantic malformed pirate version of Clark appears. It is simultaneously a Backrooms copy of his commercial persona and the story's physical expression of his self-destructive loop. It kills Clark.
16. Mary flees through rapidly changing and increasingly dreamlike spaces, including environments derived from her memories.
17. She uses the concrete handprint memento from childhood as a weapon during the chase and reaches an area where gas/canisters and environmental hazards can interrupt the creature.
18. Async personnel in protective suits capture/recover Mary and the monster.
19. Phil interrogates Mary. Async is described as a former MRI-equipment company that discovered the Backrooms and redirected itself toward studying them, with a suggested relationship between minds/brain activity and the space.
20. Final implication: the Backrooms have begun reproducing Mary's memories and have produced a distorted version of Mary. Her physical escape does not mean the space is finished with her.

## Visual rules for the game
- The default yellow rooms must be bright, flat and banal. Darkness is contrast, not the baseline.
- Geometry should be almost sensible: familiar architecture with subtle wrongness, not fantasy-dungeon design.
- Incorrectly reconstructed commercial objects and furniture should feel like memory errors.
- VHS becomes a gameplay language during the Kat/Bobby expedition, not merely a fullscreen filter.
- Later spaces can mutate into suburban interiors, display rooms, pool-like spaces, festive rooms and copied domestic memories.
- Pirate Clark should be held back. Silhouette, sound, displaced props and partial glimpses precede the full reveal.
